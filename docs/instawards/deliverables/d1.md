# D1 — Real-DEX swapper

> **Statement of Work, Deliverable 1.** Integrate the existing swapper contract end to end so
> that it performs a real swap through the Soroswap testnet router, expose it in the visual
> builder, and produce a verifiable swap transaction. Full wording in the
> [Statement of Work](../../instawards-phase-1-sow.md), section 4.1.

**Target week:** 1 (7–13 September) · **Status:** Live on
[paiflow.xyz](https://paiflow.xyz) — contract, pipeline, builder and simulation preview are
deployed to the public app and verified there, and a swap flow deployed from the public app has
swapped through the Soroswap router on testnet. The screen recording is the one item left.
Updated 10 September.

## What a user can do when this is done

Open [paiflow.xyz](https://paiflow.xyz), drag a **Swap** block onto the canvas, choose the
asset going in and the asset coming out, set a maximum slippage, deploy to Stellar testnet,
and send funds in. The contract swaps them on a real decentralised exchange and forwards the
result to the next step in the flow. Every step is verifiable on stellar.expert.

## Where it stands on 9 September

- **Merged to `develop`:** the swapper contract calling the Soroswap router (7 Sep), the
  pipeline plumbing that compiles a swap block into it (7 Sep), the builder work that makes the
  block visible and configurable (9 Sep), and the simulation preview — a live Soroswap quote in
  the config panel and on the deploy review (9 Sep). Verified locally against the real testnet
  router: 10 XLM quotes at ~1.056 USDC with a 1 % minimum of ~1.049 USDC. The Swap block
  exists end to end in the code; nothing on the code side of D1 is open.
- **Shipped to the public app on 9 September.** `develop` was merged to `staging` and deployed.
  Two configuration steps were needed beyond the merge and are worth recording: the Soroswap
  router address has no database fallback and had to be set on the service, and the app's
  `SWAPPER` contract-template row still pointed at the June binary. Because the app reads that
  row before falling back to the environment, leaving it stale would have made the factory
  instantiate the old swapper against the new constructor and every swap deploy would have
  failed.
- **Verified on paiflow.xyz itself.** The builder spec — palette, panel, five edge-case errors,
  deploy review with the TESTNET chip and the live quote — passes 9/9 against the public app,
  and the eleven screenshots and API samples in the [evidence index](../evidence/README.md) are
  its output. The quote is live: 10 XLM answers `10564278` stroops of USDC with a `10490132`
  minimum at 1 % slippage.

  `on_receive XLM → swap → pay USDC` flow was deployed through the app's own factory in one
  `deploy_pipeline` call ([`775af303…`](https://stellar.expert/explorer/testnet/tx/775af303e24ebd7f59923544df3963cc17fa05e1db66174f38f4c3ae10670943)) and triggered twice: 50 XLM

## Scope notes

Three points where the engineering work is narrower or wider than the SOW's wording. They are
recorded here rather than by editing the SOW, which is fixed as approved.

**The router is not user-selectable.** The SOW says "router selector". The panel shows a
single, read-only option, and the actual address is supplied by the server at deployment time.
A free-text address field would let a user point a flow at an arbitrary contract, and every
existing flow would break the next time Soroswap redeploys its testnet router.

**No new asset type.** The swapper trades the assets Paiflow already supports, using the live
XLM / USDC pool. Supporting exchange-issued tokens would have touched the asset model, payment
links and notifications, and was judged too wide for a 30-day sprint.

**One output.** A swap block has at most one downstream step. Splitting a payment several ways
is the splitter block's job, and combining the two is a normal flow.
