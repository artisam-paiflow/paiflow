PAIFLOW — CONSOLIDATED ALPHA TESTING FEEDBACK
Customer feedback documentation | September 2026
Prepared: 8 October 2026

PURPOSE AND METHOD
We began with usability testing to identify barriers before wider customer
discovery through planned hackathons and developer events.
Nine participants contributed written feedback: eight external testers and
one project member who piloted the protocol. Users 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 followed
the full protocol: asynchronous recorded usability testing with on-camera
responses to prepared questions, plus written answers and screenshots.
Users 4, 5, 8 and 9 followed the quick-test protocol and supplied written
feedback and screenshots without video. User1 is the project-member pilot,
as documented in the repository cohort records.

This consolidation is based on all nine written feedback files. Participant roles and blockchain
experience vary; this is a mixed usability cohort, not a validated customer
segment. Participation was primarily to help test the app.

Source evidence folder:
https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1eco_yc1XwVxY-bI6KoJfWEiukZe-FvP3

HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT
The summary below is an interpretation of the written responses. The
appendix preserves the submitted questions and answers, including missing
ratings, wording and task-number inconsistencies. Names and email addresses
are redacted; dates, roles and feedback remain. User numbers match the Drive
folder labels and are not the repository's tester-N pseudonyms.

KEY FINDINGS AND EVIDENCE
1. Plain-English flow previews help explain payment behavior.
   All four quick testers named the English Preview as the source used to
   interpret the split. Full testers also said it matched their intended
   flow. Sources: users 1–9. This supports comprehension with a preview;
   it does not establish complete understanding of contract execution.

2. Canvas navigation remains a usability barrier.
   Six participants reported issues with dragging, zooming, scrolling,
   panel positioning or canvas navigation. Sources: users 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.

3. Wallet signing and deployment need clearer explanations.
   Four full-session participants expressed uncertainty about signing,
   deployment or what happens next. Sources: users 2, 3, 6, 7.
   Some quick-test answers also conflated deploying with paying recipients.
   Sources: users 4, 8.

4. Independent task completion is uneven.
   Three quick testers reported enabling Deploy; one did not. Reported
   times were 30, 5 and 10 minutes for the three who did. Sources: users
   4, 5, 8, 9. These are self-reports for a quick-test flow, not measured
   completion times for every flow or proof of a two-minute setup claim.

5. Real-money trust remains conditional or mixed.
   Users 1 and 2 explicitly declined real-money use at this stage; users
   6 and 7 made trust conditional on security. Users 4 and 8 requested
   evidence of safety/security in their recommendation explanations.
   User3 explicitly expressed willingness to trust the app. These answers
   do not demonstrate verified security, real-money adoption or demand.

6. Mobile payment guidance needs improvement.
   Three of four quick testers were unsure how to proceed with payment.
   Sources: users 4, 5, 8. Mobile layout and zoom concerns also appeared.
   Sources: users 5, 8, 9.

7. Error handling works for some users but not consistently.
   Several full testers found validation useful, while user2 struggled
   with a technical refusal message. User1 requested clearer identification
   of the recipient missing a trustline. Sources: users 1, 2, 3, 6, 7.

IMPLICATIONS FOR CUSTOMER DEVELOPMENT
Supported: previews can help users interpret flows, and participants report
building working testnet flows.
Partially supported: the visual builder is accessible to some participants,
but navigation, mobile interactions and authorization comprehension remain
barriers.
Not yet validated: demand from the proposed customer segment, repeat use
beyond testing, willingness to pay, and readiness to transact real funds.
Suggested priorities: improve canvas navigation, clarify deployment versus
execution and wallet signing, make errors actionable, and simplify mobile
payment guidance. These are recommendations derived from feedback, not a
claim that particular changes have already shipped in response to it.

T1 — Before you clicked DEPLOY TO TESTNET, what did you think was about to happen? Could you tell what Freighter was asking you to sign?
I expected the contract to be deployed to testnet. The Review & Deploy page also clearly explained what was going to happen before I signed, including the English Preview, the pipeline architecture, the contracts that would be created, and the network being used. Because of that, I already had a clear idea of what I was about to deploy. Yes, I also understood what Freighter was asking me to sign because I already have experience with crypto and blockchain transactions.

T4 — First flow you built from scratch. Where did you hesitate?
I did not hesitate at any point while building the flow.
T5 — Did you read the English Preview before deploying? Did it match what you'd built?
Yes. I read the English Preview before deploying, and it accurately matched the flow I had built.
T6 — The payment was refused. Did you understand why, and what to do next?
Yes, I understood why because I already have knowledge about trustlines. However, I think new users may get confused because they may not know what a trustline is or how to fix the issue. It would be better if the error were clearly labeled and included simple instructions explaining what went wrong and how the user can resolve it.
T7 — What did you set out to build? Did it work? What was missing?
I created a flow using a Receive trigger followed by a Split action with three recipients. At first, it did not work because one of the recipients did not have the required trustline. What I think is missing is clearer identification of which specific recipient has the problem. The system should clearly show which recipient needs to configure or add a trustline so the user can resolve the issue immediately.
4. CLOSING QUESTIONS
1. In your own words, what does Paiflow do?
Paiflow simplifies blockchain transactions by allowing users to automate transaction flows instead of manually performing each transaction one by one.
2. Would you trust Paiflow with real money today? What would have to change?
Not yet. Paiflow is still in its early stage and currently operates on testnet. Before I would trust it with real money, I would want to see a more mature and thoroughly tested mainnet version with stronger reliability, clearer error handling, and a proven track record of successful transactions.
3. 0-10, how likely are you to recommend Paiflow? [ 7 ]
Why that number, and not one higher?
I would rate it a 7 because I can already see its value, especially for people who use crypto for payments, payroll, or repeated transactions. It can significantly simplify and automate these processes. I would rate it higher once the UI, error handling, and overall user experience become more polished and beginner-friendly.
4. What did you like most?
I liked the Split transaction feature the most because it allows me to send funds to multiple people with a single signing instead of creating separate transactions for every recipient.
5. What should we fix or improve first?
The UI should be improved first, especially the error labels and instructions. Errors should clearly explain what went wrong, where the problem occurred, and what the user needs to do to fix it.
6. What one feature would make Paiflow significantly more useful?
I would like a feature that can automatically split an incoming asset into different assets based on percentages. For example, when the flow receives XLM, it could automatically swap 50% into USDC, 30% into another asset, and 20% into another asset. This could also be useful for automated DCA strategies.
7. How comfortable were you with blockchain before today? Did that change?
I was already very comfortable with blockchain before testing Paiflow, so my level of comfort did not significantly change.

None.

Yes, [email redacted]
